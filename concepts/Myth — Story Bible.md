# Myth — Story Bible
## by Nick Rabone

14th Novemeber 2026

## Premise

Autumn 1992. Sally Miller, a 25-year-old music journalist from Warwick, is driving home from a gig in Wales when her red Mini dies on a back road in the Welsh Marches. A storm is breaking. There is no phone box, and the only light for miles comes from an old house at the end of a muddy track through dead woods.

The house belongs to the Keeper, the last person who remembered the old tales. The Keeper is gone. Every door in the house opens onto one of those tales, and every tale is drowning: a grey water of forgetting is rising through them from below, and whatever it covers is lost for ever.

Sally steps through the first door and finds herself in the land of the dead. To get home, she has to climb out of every underworld the house holds, carrying each tale back with her. Each level is one episode of a myth, told as faithfully as the sources allow.

The tone is played straight: storm, rain, lightning and dread. The myths are serious, and so is the danger.

## Sally Miller

Sally is the reason the house lets her in: she writes stories for a living. Her notebook is the game's journal. Each level she clears writes that tale into its pages, so a full notebook at the end means every tale is saved.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Born | 1967, so 25 in 1992 |
| Home | Warwick |
| Job | Music journalist |
| Car | Red Mini Cooper, white roof, registration K732 LNT (K plates ran from August 1992, which fits) |
| Look | Brunette, hair half up, red-and-black flannel shirt over a band T-shirt, ripped jeans, black Doc Martens, a patched rucksack |
| Personality | Strong-minded, says what she thinks |
| Loves | Grunge: Nirvana, Pearl Jam, Soundgarden, Alice in Chains, Stone Temple Pilots |

How she plays off the myths:

- **She talks back.** Heroes in myth obey the gods. Sally argues with them. Her one-line reactions on the intro and outro cards give the game its human voice against the seriousness of the tales.
- **She's the modern thing in an ancient world.** Flannel and Doc Martens on the banks of the Styx are the visual hook of every screen.
- **The Walkman is her anchor.** In the house hub, a muffled grunge riff plays from her headphones. Through a door, it cuts to silence, wind and that zone's sound.
- **Concept-art line:** "Same roads. Different worlds." It works as the game's tagline.

The Mini's plate is K732 LNT everywhere, including the opening screen.

## The House of Lost Tales

The house is the hub, in the spirit of Jet Set Willy's mansion. Each zone is a door; each floor opens when the one below is done. Within a floor, Sally picks the order.

**The Keeper.** An old scholar of languages and legends, never seen alive, only read. Who he really is gets its own section below. His notes, letters and margin scribbles are found in each room and explain what that door holds. The frame borrows the shape of Tolkien's *Book of Lost Tales*, a traveller who reaches a remote house and hears the forgotten legends, without borrowing its names.

**The house sits on the roots.** Its cellar opens onto the roots of the World Tree, Yggdrasil, which in Norse myth hold every world together. Each door is a place where a root touches one mythology. That is why one house can open onto Greece, Egypt and Pohjola, and it sets up the finale.

| Floor | Doors | Levels | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground floor | Greek, Egyptian | 8 + 4 | The oldest written myths, and the two great underworlds: the way in |
| First floor | Irish (Celtic), Welsh | 6 + 3 | The Otherworld under the hills; Wales is just over the border from the house |
| North wing (the library) | Anglo-Saxon, Germanic, Finnish, Slavic | 3 + 3 + 5 + 3 | The northern myths, and the ones Tolkien built from |
| Cellar | Norse | 5 | The roots of Yggdrasil, where the forgetting comes from |

That is 40 levels across 9 zones, matching the draft.

## The Keeper

The Keeper is **Mímir**, the Norse guardian of wisdom, living in 1992 as an elderly English professor. No figure in the myths fits the house better.

- **He guards a well under the roots of Yggdrasil.** In the *Prose Edda* (*Gylfaginning* 15), Mímir's well of wisdom lies beneath one of the World Tree's roots. The house sits on those roots, so his well is in the cellar.
- **His name is tied to memory.** It's usually linked with old words for remembering, so a game about forgetting has a keeper called, in effect, "the Rememberer".
- **He is only a head.** In *Ynglinga saga*, the Vanir behead Mímir. Odin preserves the head with herbs and charms, and it goes on speaking wisdom; in *Völuspá* Odin consults it as the world ends. That gives the game its darkest reveal.

**In 1992.** He has lived among people for centuries under anglicised names. To the village he's an old professor who retired to the big house decades ago: tweed, a waistcoat, a pipe, ink-stained fingers, every wall lined with books in dead languages. He's the archetype of the Oxford philologist, a nod to the scholars who rescued these myths, without being a portrait of any real person. Name options: **Professor Ambrose Mimmer**, **Dr Edmund Mymer**, or plain **Old Mr Mimms**, as the village calls him.

**How he appears.**

1. **Never alive on screen.** He appears through letters, margin notes and a portrait in the hall. One note on his desk reads: "The one who writes will come." Sally's breakdown was not chance.
2. **The reveal in the Norse zone.** The draft's Well of Mímir level already has Mímir's ancient head guarding the pool. When Sally reaches it, she finds the old professor's face, preserved and still speaking. The Keeper has been here all along, and what's left of him is guarding the last of the memory.
3. **His voice in the finale.** At the roots, the head speaks to her for the first time, and only Sally can carry the well-water he can no longer fetch.

**His correspondents.** Other keepers of memory from the zones can appear as letters in his study and as echoes in their own zones:

| Figure | Zone | Why they fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mnemosyne | Greek | Goddess of memory. Orphic gold tablets tell the dead to avoid the spring of forgetting and drink from Memory's lake instead: the exact opposite of Lethe |
| Thoth | Egyptian | Scribe of the gods, who records the verdict at the Weighing of the Heart |
| Fintan mac Bóchra | Irish | Survived the Flood and lived for millennia as a salmon, an eagle and a hawk, remembering all of Ireland's history |
| Taliesin | Welsh | The bard who claims to have been present through all the ages, and whose poem is your Battle of the Trees |
| Väinämöinen | Finnish | At the end of the *Kalevala* he sails away, promising to return when he's needed |

## The Grey Water and the World-Gnawer

The enemy is forgetting, and it has a source. Beneath the house, the dragon Níðhöggr gnaws at the roots of Yggdrasil, exactly as in the Norse poems. Where it bites, the roots bleed a grey water that rises up through every door. In Greece they call it Lethe, the river the dead drink to forget their lives. Each culture has its own name for it.

This gives one antagonist that every zone can show in its own terms, and it matches the concept art: Níðhöggr at the roots, Sally and the Mini dwarfed beneath it.

| Zone | How the forgetting shows itself | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greek | Lethe, the river of forgetfulness in Hades | Plato, *Republic* X; Virgil, *Aeneid* VI |
| Norse | Níðhöggr gnawing the roots; Odin fears his raven Muninn ("memory") will not come back | *Grímnismál* 20, 35 |
| Egyptian | The second death: a name erased is a soul destroyed; Apophis tries to swallow the sun into nothing | *Book of the Dead*; *Amduat* |
| Irish | The Otherworld folk withdraw under the hills; Oisín returns from Tír na nÓg to find the Fianna forgotten | *Lebor Gabála Érenn*; the Oisín lays |
| Welsh | Cantre'r Gwaelod, a whole kingdom drowned under Cardigan Bay | *Black Book of Carmarthen* |
| Anglo-Saxon | The elegies' lament for what is gone: "Hwær cwom mearg? Hwær cwom mago?" (Where is the horse gone? Where the young man?) | *The Wanderer*; *The Ruin* |
| Germanic | The Nibelung hoard sunk in the Rhine and lost for ever | *Nibelungenlied* |
| Finnish | Väinämöinen lacks the three lost words of power and must dig them out of the dead giant Antero Vipunen | *Kalevala*, cantos 16–17 |
| Slavic | "Dead water" and "living water" from the folk tales; the grey water is dead water | Afanasyev's tale collections |

How it plays:

- **A tile and a threat.** Grey Water replaces the blue water tile in most zones. In some levels it rises over time.
- **Colour drains near it.** Tiles and backdrops grey out the closer they are to the waterline. Saving a level floods the screen with colour again, a cheap effect that says "remembered".
- **It's never a boss until the end.** In each zone the immediate enemy is that myth's own monster. The forgetting is the tide behind them all.

## The Descent

Every zone passes through its land of the dead. That is the thread that makes nine mythologies feel like one journey: each culture's underworld is a different face of the same grey water. Your draft already has an underworld in almost every zone.

| Zone | Land of the dead | Level in the draft | Shape of the zone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greek | Hades, Tartarus | 1 Gates of the Underworld, 3 Crag of Tartarus | Start at the bottom, climb out into the living myths |
| Egyptian | The Duat | 1 Hall of Two Truths, 2 Night Barque of Ra | Judgement, the night journey, resurrection, then the climb to the stars. Already in the right order |
| Irish | The Sídhe, the hollow hills | 6 Great Hall of the Dagda | Surface tales first, end under the hill |
| Welsh | Annwn | 3 Trial of Annwn | Arawn of Annwn is the enemy in Cad Goddeu too, so the zone builds to his realm |
| Anglo-Saxon | Grendel's mere, the dragon's barrow | 1 Haunted Mere, 2 Dragon's Barrow | Down into the water, then into the grave |
| Germanic | The doomed hall | 3 Burning Hall of Etzel | The Burgundians ride knowingly to their deaths: a descent by choice |
| Finnish | Tuonela | 3 River of Tuonela | In the middle: the zone dips into death and comes back out |
| Slavic | Nav, the land beyond the forest | 2 Baba Yaga's Clearing | Her hut on chicken legs stands at the border of the living and the dead |
| Norse | Helheim, then the roots | 5 Halls of Helheim | The last descent of the game, leading into the finale |

The through-line for Sally: she starts the game in Hades, and she ends it at the very bottom of everything, at the roots under the house. Each zone in between is a smaller version of that same fall and climb.

A few beats worth adding:

- **Greek, an Orpheus level.** "The Way Up": a long climb out of Hades where turning back sends you down. It fits between Tartarus and the Labyrinth. Greek can carry nine levels.
- **A Keeper's note per zone.** One page in the zone's first room sets up the myth in a few lines, in the Keeper's voice, before Sally steps through.
- **Sally's reaction card per level.** One line on exit. It's where her personality lives.

## Finale and epilogue

**The finale: the Roots of Yggdrasil.** With the Norse zone saved, the cellar door opens. Below it, the roots of the World Tree fill a black chasm over Niflheim. The road has fallen in too: Sally's Mini sits wedged among the roots, headlights still on. The forgetting has reached her world. This is the concept piece, *Sally meets Níðhöggr*, made playable.

The level is the draft's Roots level, scaled up:

- **The roots rot and crumble** as Níðhöggr gnaws.
- **Sally carries white water from the Well of Urðr** to heal them. In the *Prose Edda*, the Norns pour this water on the tree every day to keep it from rotting.
- **Níðhöggr lunges** up from the chasm on a rhythm.
- **She places the nine saved tales**, one from each zone, at the Norns' loom.

**She doesn't kill the dragon.** In *Völuspá*, Níðhöggr is still flying at the end of the world, so killing it would break the myth. Instead the roots heal, the grey water drains, and the dragon sinks back into the dark to gnaw another day. That's faithful, and it fits the dread.

**Epilogue.** Dawn. The storm has gone. Sally is sitting in the Mini on the verge of the back road. She turns the key, and it starts. The Walkman clicks on mid-song. In the mirror, the track behind her leads to a ruin that clearly hasn't had a roof for a hundred years. On the passenger seat is her notebook, every page full.

The final card is her article in print, with the byline *by Sally Miller*. Under it, the last line of the notebook from the concept art: "Maybe this is just the beginning..."

Open question:

Was it real? The full notebook says yes. Do we leave that ambiguous, or does a final detail settle it, like a golden apple on the dashboard?

## Faithfulness Notes & Corrections

The draft was mostly true to its sources. These are the places where it drifts, given the aim of staying as close to the stories as possible. All of these fixes are now applied in Revised level entries, below. They're written from knowledge of the texts; the Finnish Sun and Moon entry was checked against Crawford's translation of canto 49.

| Level | Where it drifts | Suggested fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greek 1, Gates of the Underworld | Obols paid Charon for the crossing; they weren't put on scales. Weighing scales are Egyptian | Spend obols on Charon's ferry, a moving platform. Keep the pomegranate seeds: they're Persephone's |
| Greek 2, Labyrinth | Ariadne's thread is the way back out, not a trap | Make the thread the guide: follow it to find the exit, and it frays if the Minotaur crosses it |
| Greek 4, Medusa | Ovid puts Medusa's curse in Athena's temple, but Perseus finds her at the Gorgons' home at the edge of the world | Fine if the level is framed as the cursed temple; otherwise rename it for the Gorgons' isle |
| Greek 7, Sirens | Harpies belong to the Argonauts' story (Phineus), not the *Odyssey* | Swap for Scylla's reaching heads, or keep harpies as a Greek 8 hazard if the Argonautica gets a level |
| Norse 3 and 4 | Gjallarhorn is the goal of both levels | Chain them: fetch it at Mímir's well (it's kept there in the *Prose Edda*), then sound it on Bifröst |
| Norse 5, Helheim | Hel's price is that everything weeps for Baldr; the tears aren't golden | Use "tears of the world", or Freyja's tears of red gold as the collectible, which are genuinely Norse |
| Finnish 1, Sampo | The furnace's failed creations are a crossbow, a boat, a heifer and a plough (canto 10). The golden maiden is a different episode (canto 37) | Use the canto 10 four as the patrolling enemies |
| Finnish 2, Singing Duel | The first kantele is made from the pike's jawbone in canto 40, long after the duel in canto 3 | Change the goal: collect the words of power, and Joukahainen offers his sister Aino to be freed from the bog |
| Finnish 4, Sun and Moon | The draft's "Mount Kiimavaara" and "nine locks" aren't in the poem: Louhi hides the Sun and Moon in Pohjola's copper-bearing mountain, behind iron-banded doors (canto 47). The nine locks guard the Sampo instead | Rename it The Copper Mountain of Pohjola, and use Ilmarinen's collar for the goal (canto 49) |
| Egyptian 3, Osiris | Canopic jars are tomb equipment, not part of the myth. Isis gathers the pieces of Osiris's body (Plutarch) | Collect the scattered pieces as relic-shrines, handled with care |
| Irish 5, Tara | The Four Treasures are the Lia Fáil itself, the Spear of Lugh, the Sword of Nuada and the Dagda's Cauldron. There's no shield | Collect the three treasures and bring them to the Stone, which is the fourth |
| Irish 6, Dagda | His harp plays three strains: sorrow, joy and sleep | Make it three strains, each shifting the blocks a different way |
| Welsh 3, Annwn | The *Preiddeu Annwfn* cauldron is the Chief of Annwn's, which won't boil a coward's food. The Cauldron of Rebirth is a different one, from *Branwen* | Rename it the Cauldron of the Chief of Annwn |
| Germanic 1, Linden Leaf | Balmung isn't shattered and reforged in the *Nibelungenlied*; that's the Norse Gram | Collect pieces of the Nibelung hoard, or the cloak of invisibility (Tarnkappe), instead |
| Germanic 2, Lorelei | The Lorelei is 19th-century literature (Brentano, then Heine), not old legend | Rework it as Hagen sinking the hoard in the Rhine, or the Danube water-women who foretell the Burgundians' doom. Both are in the *Nibelungenlied* |
| Slavic 3, World Oak | Perun versus Veles is a modern scholarly reconstruction from folklore fragments, not a surviving text | Fine to use, but the Keeper's note should say it's pieced together, which suits the theme |

Things the draft gets right that are worth keeping: Scáthach's bridge that throws you off (it really does in the tale), Lemminkäinen's mother raking the river for his pieces, the seventh hour of the *Amduat* as Apophis's hour, the Sampo's four ingredients, and the Children of Lir's three exiles.

## Revised level entries

These replace the matching entries in the level outlines draft. Every other level stands as written.

### Greek

**1. The Gates of the Underworld (Hades and Cerberus)**

- **Screen Layout:** As drafted: an obsidian palace hall over the Styx, bronze gates and tiered marble balconies. A white cypress marks the spring of Lethe, which runs as Grey Water.
- **Core Mechanic:** Collect silver obols and pay Charon at the jetty. His ferry carries Sally across the Styx on a fixed route, a moving platform.
- **Hazards and Enemies:** Shades drift up and down fixed shafts. Cerberus lets the dead in but stops them leaving, so he only lunges when Sally heads towards the gates.
- **Level Goal:** Collect three pomegranate seeds, Persephone's fruit, and offer them at the thrones of Hades and Persephone to unseal the gates.

**2. The Labyrinth of Knossos (The Minotaur)**

- **Core Mechanic:** Ariadne's thread unspools behind Sally from the entrance. The only way out is to follow it back. If the Minotaur charges across it, the thread breaks and she must find the loose end.
- **Level Goal:** Gather four bronze double axes (labrys) from the dead-end alcoves, then follow the thread back to the entrance.
- Layout and hazards as drafted.

**4. The Isle of the Gorgons (Medusa)** *(renamed from The Temple of Athena)*

- **Screen Layout:** A ruined temple on the Gorgons' isle at the edge of the world, full of lifelike statues of those who looked. The ruin echoes Ovid's telling, in which Medusa was cursed in Athena's temple.
- **Level Goal:** Retrieve the gifts Perseus was given: the polished shield (Athena), the winged sandals (the nymphs) and the Helm of Darkness (Hades).
- Mechanic and hazards as drafted.

**7. The Strait of Anthemoessa (The Sirens)**

- **Hazards and Enemies:** Crashing waves sweep the lower wreckage. Scylla's six heads reach down from a cliff cave at fixed points, as in *Odyssey* XII. The harpies are gone; they belong to the Argonauts' story.
- **Level Goal:** As drafted: four coils of rope bound to the mast, as Odysseus was bound. Optional: beeswax plugs, like his crew's, cancel the song's inverted controls for a few seconds.

### Norse

**3. The Crossing of Bifröst (Heimdall's Watch)**

- **Level Goal:** Carry Gjallarhorn, brought from Mímir's well in the previous level, up the shifting bands to Heimdall's hall and sound it.
- Layout, mechanic and hazards as drafted.

**4. The Well of Mímir (Odin's Sacrifice)** *(now played before Bifröst)*

- **Level Goal:** Recover Gjallarhorn, which is kept at the well in the *Prose Edda*. In the pool lies Odin's pledged eye, and beside it the face of the Keeper: the reveal (see The Keeper).
- Layout, mechanic and hazards as drafted.

**5. The Halls of Helheim (The Crossing of Gjöll)**

- **Level Goal:** Hel will release Baldr only if everything weeps for him. Collect three tears wept by stone, tree and metal, which all wept in *Gylfaginning*, and bring them to Hel's throne. A fourth tear, belonging to the giantess Þökk, can be seen but never reached, because she refused to weep. The exit opens, but Baldr stays: faithful, and grim.

### Finnish

**1. The Forging of the Sampo (Ilmarinen's Smithy)**

- **Hazards and Enemies:** The furnace's failed creations patrol the walkways until quenched: a crossbow, a boat, a heifer and a plough (canto 10).

**2. The Singing Duel (Väinämöinen and Joukahainen)** *(now the zone's first level)*

- **Level Goal:** Väinämöinen sings Joukahainen deeper into the bog, and Joukahainen offers ransom after ransom to be freed. Collect the four ransoms he offers: a crossbow, a boat, a stallion and gold. When the last is gathered, he promises his sister Aino, the singing stops and a stone causeway rises. That promise drives the tragedy that follows in the poem.
- No kantele: the first one isn't made until canto 40.

**4. The Copper Mountain of Pohjola (The Sun and the Moon)** *(renamed from Mount Kiimavaara)*

- **Screen Layout:** The dark interior of Pohjola's copper-bearing mountain, with iron-banded cavern doors, icicles and narrow shafts.
- **Core Mechanic and Hazards:** As drafted: near-darkness with a small light around Sally, flint-struck torches, frost wraiths and falling icicles.
- **Level Goal:** Gather the master-keys and iron rings Ilmarinen forges. When the collar is complete, Louhi, in the shape of a black eagle, sees it and flies home in fear to unlock the doors herself. The Sun and Moon flood the screen with light.

**5. The Great Pike of Pohjola** *(note only)*

- This combines two episodes: the pike on the voyage to Pohjola (canto 40) and Louhi's eagle attack on the way home with the Sampo (canto 43). Keep the merger, but frame it as one voyage there and back.

### Egyptian

**3. The Tomb of Osiris (The Fourteen Pieces)**

- **Level Goal:** Gather the scattered pieces of Osiris, each kept in a small reliquary shrine, and bring them to the bier where Isis and Nephthys keep vigil. Their lament wakes him and opens the tomb.

### Irish

**5. The Hill of Tara (The Lia Fáil)**

- **Level Goal:** Bring two of the Four Treasures, the Spear of Lugh and the Sword of Nuada, to the Lia Fáil, the Stone of Destiny and the third treasure, which roars when they're set before it. The fourth, the Dagda's Cauldron, waits in the next level, so the two levels link.

**6. The Great Hall of the Dagda (The Cauldron of Plenty)**

- **Core Mechanic:** The Dagda's harp plays three strains, sorrow, joy and sleep, as in *Cath Maige Tuired*. Each strain shifts the solid and ghostly blocks a different way.
- **Level Goal:** Play the three strains in turn, then claim the Cauldron, completing the Four Treasures.

### Welsh

**3. The Trial of Annwn (The Spoils of the Otherworld)**

- **Level Goal:** Claim the Cauldron of the Chief of Annwn: rimmed with pearls, warmed by the breath of nine maidens, and refusing to boil a coward's food. Carry it back to the entrance. The poem's refrain, that only seven came back, can open and close the level.

### Germanic

**1. The Linden Leaf (Fafnir's Lair)**

- **Level Goal:** Claim the three prizes Siegfried took from the Nibelungs: the sword Balmung, the Tarnkappe cloak of invisibility, and the key to the hoard. Nothing is shattered or reforged; that belongs to the Norse Sigurd.

**2. The Hoard Beneath the Rhine** *(replaces The Crag of the Lorelei)*

After Siegfried's murder, Hagen sinks the Nibelung hoard in the Rhine so Kriemhild can never use it, and it is lost for ever. It's the zone's purest image of forgetting.

- **Screen Layout:** Steep slate cliffs over the dark Rhine at night, with weathered docks and a river barge.
- **Core Mechanic:** As drafted: currents pull Sally towards the edge, and timed jumps fight the pull. The pull now comes from the river itself.
- **Hazards and Enemies:** River swells and sliding slate, as drafted.
- **Level Goal:** Carry three chests of the hoard down to the barge and sink them before Kriemhild's men arrive.

### Slavic

**3. The Roots of the World Oak (Perun and Veles)**

- No gameplay change. The Keeper's note for this level says the myth has been pieced together from fragments, which suits a game about lost tales.

## Build notes

About ten reusable systems cover nearly every mechanic in the 40 levels. Build those once and most levels become a reskin plus one twist, the same approach that kept The ULA Contention manageable.

| System | What it is | Levels that use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moving platforms | Platforms on a path or tied to a trigger | Charon's ferry, Etna's gears, Wayland's cages, the Egyptian scales, the Great Pike's breathing back, Scáthach's tilting bridge |
| Phasing blocks | Solid for a while, then not, on a timer or a switch | Bifröst, the Dagda's harps, Tara's moonlight, the Hesperides' starlight, Annwn's torch bridges, Unas's sand-clock stairs |
| Crumbling and sinking | Already built as mud; extend with variants | Medusa's pillars, Moyle's ice, Segais's lily pads, the Singing Duel's hummocks, the Roots' decay, Arachne's threads |
| Rising liquid | A level-wide line that climbs | Cantre'r Gwaelod's tide, the Sampo's magma, the Grey Water, Etzel's spreading fire |
| Force zones | Wind, current, pull or inverted controls | Moyle's gale, the Rhine, Osiris's sandstorm, Segais's currents, the Sirens' song, the Sampo's hot air |
| Switches | Levers, gongs, plates and harps that flip a state | Etna's gongs, Cantre'r Gwaelod's levers, Culann's bellows, Wayland's winches, Tara's plates, Osiris's obelisks |

**The Greek zone needs, in order:** moving platforms (Charon), carry and place (the seeds), the lunge guardian (Cerberus), drifting shades, then emitters. Greek's one-off twists are Sisyphus's rolling boulder, Ariadne's thread, Medusa's sweeping gaze, the Sirens' inverted controls and Arachne's springy threads.

**Fonts.** Almendra is the title font: it reads as old and carved without being a cliché. VT323 is a crisp pixel terminal font, which suits the HUD, numbers and Sally's 1992 voice. Decided: Almendra for the title, the Keeper's notes and the level intro cards; VT323 for the HUD, menus and Sally's lines.
